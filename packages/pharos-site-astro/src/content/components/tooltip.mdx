## Usage

### Placement

There are 12 placement options available. Tooltips can be placed top, bottom, left or right
of the target (e.g. button, icon, link, etc.) and the caret (triangle) can be placed at the
start, center or end of the tooltip.

#### Where should I place the tooltip?

All tooltips, by default, are positioned top-center.

You can also decide to position the tooltip in a more appropriate place depending on the
use case.

##### Navigational

For navigational items, the tooltip should be placed to the left of the associated icon
button.

##### Icon toolbar

For content viewing experiences, the tooltip should sit below the viewer control icon
buttons.

##### Icon buttons

For tooltips that are near other objects or items, display the tooltip to have more
contrast, prominence and readability. In the example below, the tooltip has a top
placement.

##### Longer titles

For titles that need a tooltip to display the full title, it should sit above the
content, so the link text is still visible to the user.

##### Caret placement

We use <site-pharos-link href="https://floating-ui.com/">Floating UI</site-pharos-link> to
position our tooltips. By default, tooltips are placed in the center of the trigger, but the
caret/arrow will move and remain "attached" with Floating UI's flip and shift functionality.

### Longer content

Larger tooltips can be used to display further additional information. It is highly
recommended that you consider if you need a tooltip or not for the design. "Throw it in a
tooltip" is not the answer.

## Best practices

<BestPractices>
  <Fragment slot="do">
    - Use tooltips to clarify expected behavior when interacting with icon buttons, buttons, or
      links with an associated keyboard shortcut.
    - Use tooltips sparingly. If you're building something that requires a lot of tooltips, work
      on clarifying the design and the language in the experience. Do you NEED a tooltip?
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="dont">
    - Don't include interactive content such as buttons or links.
    - Don't include imagery, concise text will do.
    - Don't use tooltips to restate visible text in the UI.
    - Don't use tooltips to communicate critical information, like errors or other interaction
      feedback.
  </Fragment>
</BestPractices>

## Content guidelines

<em>Tooltips should:</em>

- Be concise and scannable
- Be written in sentence case
- Not be used to communicate error messages or critical info

## Accessibility

### Relevant WCAG guidelines

- [1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus AA](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html)

### Importance

Tooltips give additional information to the user by providing information that isn't presented
visually. Tooltips can take multiple forms, such as a label for an icon (similar to our metadata
icon on the item page) or providing additional information or clarification about an element (usually
in the form of a "i" or a "?" icon). Tooltips do not need to receive focus, but rather just live
on the button, link or form field that it relates to. There may be additional Aria tags that need
to be added to make the tooltip available to assistive technologies, such as aria-describedby.

### Expected actions

#### Screen reader

- Reads similarly to a text label
- Announces the tooltip on hover/focus

#### Keyboard

- Pressing <kbd>Escape</kbd> dismisses the tooltip.
- Should be included in tab order logically
- Tooltip should open when focus is given to the element
- Tooltip should not automatically disappear
- After tooltip is closed, focus should remain on the trigger
